## Brief (from the kit's RULES.txt, judge brief in full)

Write one sentence, up to 200 characters, an absurd clause hidden deep inside
a terms-of-service agreement. A judge model compares clauses pairwise and the
funniest wins. The judge weighs, most important first:

1. Is an absurd clause that could hide deep inside a terms-of-service
   agreement.
2. Is funny on its own, with a joke that lands on one reading and needs no
   setup from outside the line.
3. Sounds like real legal boilerplate until the absurdity hits, so the joke
   comes from the register as much as from the words.
4. Is original and specific, with a concrete absurd image rather than a vague
   shrug at the genre.
5. Is tight and well written, one clean sentence with no wasted words and no
   explaining of its own joke.

"A clause that only works once the reader already knows it is a joke is weaker
than one that ambushes them. Length for its own sake does not count."

## Authoring prompt used in this session

Write six candidate clauses in English, each one sentence of at most 200
characters, each written as genuine legal boilerplate — numbered clauses,
"the Company", "perpetual, irrevocable", "(1)" numerals, "thirty (30) days" —
until the absurdity ambushes the reader. The absurd image must be concrete
(a goat, a sock, a shadow, a dream) and the clause must be funny with no setup
outside the line. Rank them against the five judge criteria above, pick the
strongest, then revise it once with a real change to the text (not cosmetics)
before finalizing.
